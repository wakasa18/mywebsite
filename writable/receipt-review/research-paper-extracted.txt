[0013] Introduction
[0014] In recent years, the increasing demand for health services has placed significant pressure on pharmacies to efficiently manage sales, maintain accurate inventory records, and anticipate customer needs. To address these challenges, many pharmacies have adopted integrated online point of sale and inventory management systems with sales forecasting capabilities to enable real-time sales recording, automatic inventory updates, and data-driven decision-making. Studies comparing forecasting methods such as naive, moving average, exponential smoothing, and Holt-Winters have shown that analyzing historical sales data improves prediction accuracy and helps minimize supply imbalances (Burinskiene, 2022).
[0015] Analyzing past sales trends allows pharmacies to maintain appropriate stock levels, reduce shortages, and avoid excess inventory. This is why effective inventory management plays an important role in helping small and medium-sized retail businesses maintain correct stock levels and avoid stockouts (Wilson, 2022). In the Philippines, small community pharmacies increasingly recognize the importance of effective inventory management systems in ensuring product availability and improving customer satisfaction (Soliveres et al., 2024).
[0016] Despite these developments, many small community pharmacies still rely on manual inventory processes, which can lead to inefficiencies and inaccuracies in procurement planning. Manual recording and estimation often result in overstocking or medicine shortages, both of which negatively affect operational efficiency (Yulianti et al., 2025). Although pharmacies commonly use traditional inventory practices such as Economic Order Quantity, First Expired, First Out, and other stock control methods, these are often done manually, making accurate stock tracking and demand prediction difficult (Soliveres et al., 2024).
[0017] Despite the growing number of studies on pharmacy information systems, there remains a lack of research that integrates point-of-sale processing, real-time inventory updating, and sales forecasting into a single system tailored for small pharmacies. Most related studies focus on either digital inventory monitoring or predictive analytics separately; however, fewer studies explain how these functions can work together to support accurate stock control, faster transaction recording, and proactive demand planning. This gap is especially evident in small community pharmacies that continue to rely on manual processes, making it difficult to maintain accurate records and anticipate future demand.
[0018] To address these concerns, integrating automated POS and inventory systems with forecasting features can improve inventory accuracy, minimize waste, prevent shortages, and support better decision-making (Divina et al., 2025). Therefore, this study aims to develop an integrated online point of sale and inventory system with sales forecasting for Pharxmaco Drugstore. By combining real-time transaction recording with predictive analytics, the proposed system seeks to improve operational performance, strengthen inventory stability, and support the digital transformation goals of Sustainable Development Goal 9.
[0019] This study also aligns with Sustainable Development Goal 9: Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure, particularly Target 9.5, which promotes technological innovation and digital adoption among small and medium-sized enterprises. By introducing an integrated online point of sale and inventory system with forecasting capabilities, the study supports digital transformation in small-scale healthcare operations. This implementation can improve operational efficiency, reduce inventory wastage, and help ensure the continuous availability of essential medicines.
[0020] The general problem of this study is the continued use of manual sales and inventory processes at Pharxmaco Drugstore, which leads to inaccurate stock records and difficulty in anticipating customer demand. To address this, the study aims to develop a system that automates sales, improves real-time inventory accuracy, and incorporates sales forecasting to enhance operational efficiency.

[0028] Sales Forecasting 
[0029] The integration of forecasting techniques in inventory systems has been identified as an effective approach in managing product demand and minimizing stock-related issues. Forecasting methods such as exponential smoothing utilize historical sales data to predict future demand and support better inventory planning (Sihotang, 2023). In pharmaceutical contexts, these forecasting techniques have proven highly effective in predicting medication demand and optimizing supply chain performance.
[0030] Sales forecasting serves as a fundamental component in predicting future demand and maintaining an optimal inventory balance. While traditional manual forecasting methods remain in common use, evidence suggests that statistical approaches provide significantly higher prediction of accuracy.
[0031] Among the most common methodologies is Simple Exponential Smoothing, which is effective for data that fluctuates around a roughly constant level and gives greater weight to recent observations than to earlier sales data (Abdelati & Abdelwali, 2024). However, Simple Exponential Smoothing often fails to respond to clear upward or downward trends, as it models only the level of the data.
[0032] To address this limitation, Holt’s Linear Exponential Smoothing extends the basic model by adding a second equation that explicitly tracks the trend, allowing for more accurate forecasting in scenarios where demand is increasing or decreasing over time. Recent research demonstrates that selecting the appropriate model is critical; while Holt’s method is generally recommended for trending data, Simple Exponential Smoothing can perform better if the trend is weak, as it avoids forcing a linear trend on volatile sales data (Kumar et al., 2024).
[0033] Furthermore, the integration of hybrid and statistical forecasting methods further enhances prediction accuracy, supporting more informed inventory decisions. Integrating these decision support systems with data mining techniques has also emerged as a robust approach for developing predictive systems that reduce waste and improve stock planning in pharmaceutical supply chains.

[0065] Scope and Delimitation
[0066] Scope of the Study
[0067] This study focuses on developing an integrated Online Point of Sale and Inventory System with Sales Forecasting for Pharxmaco Drugstore. The proposed system consolidates transaction processing, inventory control, predictive analytics, and multi-branch stock visibility into a unified online platform to help streamline pharmacy operations and support better decision-making.
[0068] The Point-of-Sale module handles sales transactions through real-time product selection, automatic computation of totals, discount application, payment processing, and digital receipt generation. It is designed to ensure accurate transaction recording, minimize human errors, and enhance overall efficiency during daily business activities.
[0069] The Inventory Management module monitors product details, stock levels, expiration dates, and stock movements in real time, with records updated automatically after each transaction. Additionally, the system provides automated low-stock and expiration alerts to help prevent shortages and minimize product wastage, which improves inventory control accuracy.
[0070] The system also supports multi-branch inventory visibility, allowing authorized users to view product availability across different branches. If a product is unavailable in one branch, the system can indicate whether the item is available in another branch, enabling staff to recommend the nearest branch where the customer may obtain the product. This feature improves customer service and supports better coordination among branches.
[0071] The Sales Forecasting module uses sample data obtained from Kaggle to test and verify the forecasting function of the system. The forecasting module applies statistical methods, specifically Holt’s Exponential Smoothing, to evaluate whether the forecasting logic produces reasonable results and functions correctly.
[0072] Delimitations of the Study
[0073] The study is limited to the functions included in the proposed system and does not cover connecting with external suppliers. It does not use actual historical sales records from Pharxmaco Drugstore for forecasting. Instead, Kaggle sample data are used only to test and verify the forecasting feature.
[0074] The forecasting module does not consider external market variables such as competitor pricing, regional health trends, or macroeconomic changes. The system also does not incorporate advanced artificial intelligence or complex machine learning models. Furthermore, the forecasting feature is restricted to a 24-month data window, which limits the depth of long-term prediction analysis.

[0098] Software Development Model
[0099] Figure 2. Agile Software Development Model
[0100] This figure  illustrates the Agile Software Development Model that will be used in the development of the proposed Online Point of Sale (POS) and Inventory System with Sales Forecasting for Pharxmaco Drugstore. Agile is an iterative development approach that will allow researchers to continuously improve the system through repeated development cycles. Each phase of the process will play an important role in ensuring that the system is properly planned, developed, tested, and refined.
[0101] The Agile model will serve as the framework for the Pharxmaco system because it enables a continuous, feedback-driven development cycle. Unlike linear models, Agile will allow the researchers to treat the system not as a single, finished product, but as an evolving solution that will be refined through repeated cycles of design, development, testing, and review. This iterative approach will ensure that each feature ranging from basic POS transactions to complex sales forecasting is built, verified, and improved based on actual pharmacy needs.
[0102] The process will function through the following integrated phases, where each iteration links these stages to ensure constant system advancement:
[0103] Planning Phase: The researchers will identify the scope, objectives, and overall coverage of the system, gathering requirements based on the problems identified in the current manual sales and inventory processes of the pharmacy.
[0104] Design Phase: The researchers will create system designs, including flowcharts, data flow diagrams, database structures, and user interface layouts, which will define how the system will function and how different components will interact.
[0105] Development Phase: The researchers will build the system using appropriate programming tools and technologies to implement core features, such as sales transaction processing, inventory management, discount handling, reporting, and sales forecasting. By focusing on one module at a time, the team will ensure that core functionalities are stable before adding more advanced capabilities.
[0106] Testing Phase: The system will be evaluated to identify errors, bugs, and performance issues. Once a specific feature is built, it will undergo immediate functional testing to create a feedback loop where results will directly inform the refinements made in the subsequent iteration.
[0107] Review Phase: The system will be assessed based on user feedback and the ISO/IEC 25010 standard. During this stage, the performance of the newly implemented features will be assessed, allowing researchers to capture user feedback early and identify necessary adjustments to improve usability or transaction speed before the next development cycle begins.
[0108] Agile has been successfully applied in similar studies involving web-based and online systems development. For example implemented an Agile-based web Point of Sale and Inventory System, allowing iterative refinement based on user feedback, which improved operational efficiency and inventory accuracy (Divina et al., 2025). Similarly, Agile strategies used to develop an online pharmacy sales and inventory system, adapting features according to actual pharmacy workflows and user requirements (Garcia et al. 2024). These studies demonstrate that Agile is effective in developing responsive, user-centered systems in small-scale retail and healthcare environments, making it a suitable model for the present study.

[0188] Simple Exponential Smoothing (SES)
[0189] Simple Exponential Smoothing (SES) is a foundational forecasting method used for data that is relatively stable and does not show a clear upward or downward trend. It works on the principle that the most recent data points are more representative of the future than older ones (Abdelati & Abdelwali, 2024). Therefore, it assigns exponentially decreasing weights to older observations, giving the most "weight" to the most recent actual sales.
[0190] In the context of a drugstore, SES would be used for products with steady, consistent demand (e.g., standard supplies like cotton or basic alcohol) where sales neither grow significantly nor decline over time.
[0191] The forecast for the next period is calculated as
[0192]                                                  Ft+1=αAt+1-αFt
[0193] Ft+1:   
[0194] At:      The actual sales of the current period.
[0195] Ft:      The previously calculated forecast for the current period.
[0196] α   (Alpha) : The smoothing constant (between 0 and 1). 
[0197] A higher alpha makes the forecast more reactive to the most recent data, while a lower alpha makes it smoother and less reactive to sudden spikes.
[0198] Sample Computation:
[0199] Assume you want to forecast sales for the next day.
[0200] Actual Sales today (At):       100 units
[0201]             Previous Forecast  (Ft):       90 units
[0202] Smoothing constant  (α   ) :   0.3
[0203] Computation:
[0204] Ft+1=0.3×100+1-0.3×90
[0205] Ft+1=30+0.7×90
[0206] Ft+1=30+63
[0207] Ft+1=93
[0208] The forecast for the next period is 93 units. Because the smoothing constant is 0.3, the model adjusted the previous forecast of 90 slightly upward toward the actual sales of 100, but did not jump all the way to 100, effectively "smoothing out" the data.
[0209]  Holt’s Exponential Smoothing
[0210] Holt’s Exponential Smoothing, also known as Double Exponential Smoothing, was selected as the forecasting method because pharmacy sales data may exhibit gradual increases or decreases over time. The method is suitable for demand forecasting in inventory systems because it can capture trend behavior in sales data (Kumar et al., 2024). This makes it more suitable for the proposed Online Point of Sale and Inventory System for Pharxmaco Drugstore with Sales Forecasting, where demand may change over time. The method remains efficient, relatively simple to implement, and supports real-time computation within the system.
[0211] The daily forecasting module processes raw daily transaction data to project demand for the following day. By accounting for recent sales trends, the system dynamically adjusts to immediate fluctuations in market activity. This high-frequency analysis is critical for monitoring fast-moving consumer goods and ensuring optimal stock availability for the pharmacy.
[0212] The weekly forecasting module uses aggregated weekly sales data to forecast future demand. By consolidating daily data into weekly intervals, the model filters out day-to-day noise and short-term volatility, allowing it to capture broader sales trends. This level of detail is useful for planning routine restocking cycles and maintaining overall inventory health.
[0213] Finally, the monthly forecasting module operates on aggregated monthly data to provide a longitudinal perspective of demand patterns. By smoothing out seasonal and weekly variances, this forecasting interval supports high level organizational objectives, including long term procurement planning, financial budgeting, and strategic resource allocation.
[0214] The system applies Holt’s Exponential Smoothing using the following formulas:
[0215] Level:Fₜ = αAₜ + (1 − α) (Fₜ₋₁ + Tₜ₋₁)
[0216] Trend:Tₜ = β (Fₜ − Fₜ₋₁) + (1 − β) Tₜ₋₁
[0217] Forecast:Fₜ₊₁ = Fₜ + Tₜ
[0218] where Fₜ represents the current level or smoothed value, Tₜ denotes the trend component, Aₜ is the actual sales, α is the smoothing constant for the level, and β is the smoothing constant for the trend. In this study, α = 0.3 and β = 0.2 were used to balance responsiveness and stability, allowing the system to adapt to recent changes while maintaining consistency in forecasting.
[0219] The initial values for the level and trend are determined using historical data. The initial level is based on the first observed sales value, while the initial trend is calculated from the difference between early data points. These initial values are necessary to start the iterative forecasting process.
[0220] To demonstrate the computation, assume that the actual sales (Aₜ) is 1,000 units, the previous level (Fₜ₋₁) is 900 units, the previous trend (Tₜ₋₁) is 50 units, α = 0.3, and β = 0.2.
[0221] Level computation: Fₜ = (0.3 × 1000) + (0.7 × (900 + 50)) Fₜ = 300 + (0.7 × 950) Fₜ = 300 + 665 Fₜ = 965
[0222] Trend computation: Tₜ = (0.2 × (965 − 900)) + (0.8 × 50) Tₜ = (0.2 × 65) + 40 Tₜ = 13 + 40 Tₜ = 53
[0223] Forecast computation: Fₜ₊₁ = 965 + 53 Fₜ₊₁ = 1,018
[0224] The computed forecast for the next period is 1,018 units. This result reflects both the current sales level and the upward trend, enabling the system to generate more accurate predictions compared to methods that do not consider trends. In the implementation of the system, actual sales data are retrieved from the database, previous level and trend values are obtained, and the formulas are applied using the defined constants. The computed forecast is then stored in the forecasting table and utilized for inventory planning and report generation.
[0225] Holt’s vs Simple Exponential Smoothing
[0226] The fundamental difference between these two methods lies in their ability to process change over time. Simple Exponential Smoothing is a univariate forecasting technique designed for stationary data, meaning it assumes that the sales figures will fluctuate around a constant average without any underlying growth or decline. It relies on a single smoothing constant to update the level, effectively weighing the most recent observation while ignoring the momentum of the data. Consequently, this method is suited for a dynamic retail environment because it consistently produces a lagging forecast that fails to capture the trajectory of sales movement.
[0227] Holt’s Exponential Smoothing, also referred to as Double Exponential Smoothing, significantly advances this capability by incorporating a secondary smoothing constant to account for a trend component. This structure allows the algorithm to explicitly model the direction and speed of change within the sales data, effectively capturing both the current level of demand and the rate at which that demand is evolving. By maintaining these two distinct parameters, the system can anticipate future values based on observed momentum rather than relying on a static historical average.
[0228] Holt’s method is the superior choice because it provides a proactive rather than a reactive forecast. Pharmacy inventory management requires high precision to prevent both stockouts and excessive inventory carrying costs or over investment in working capital. Because Holt’s method accounts for the trend, it can adjust for increasing demand during peak seasons or decreasing interest in certain products, ensuring that the replenishment logic remains aligned with actual store performance. This technical robustness ensures that the forecasting feature provides genuine operational utility, justifying its selection over the more limited Simple Exponential Smoothing model.

The orange line represents the test data, indicating the actual observed values used to evaluate the forecasting performance. The green line shows the simple forecasting method, which produces a relatively constant or linear projection and does not fully capture the upward trend present in the data. The system uses a 24-month dataset for testing and forecasting evaluation. These records contain transaction date, medicine name, quantity sold, unit price, and total sales, and they serve as the raw input for the data cleaning and forecasting procedures used in the system.
[0578] Data cleaning is necessary because raw sales records may contain irrelevant entries, duplicate records, missing values, or invalid information that can affect the accuracy of the analysis. To improve data quality, the system applies filtering to remove unnecessary records, deduplication to eliminate repeated entries, imputation to fill missing values, validation to check correctness and consistency, and chronological arrangement to organize the sales data in time order.
[0579] Data Cleaning
[0580] To ensure high data integrity and prevent errors in our predictive analytics, the system executes the following automated cleaning protocols before processing any information.
[0581] Filtering - The system removes irrelevant or unnecessary sales records to ensure that only useful historical sales data are included for analysis.
[0582] Deduplication - The system scans the sales records for repeated entries and removes duplicates so that each sale is counted only once.
[0583] Imputation - The system identifies missing or incomplete sales values and fills them using appropriate replacement values to maintain completeness and ensure that the historical sales data remain suitable for forecasting.
[0584] Validation - The system checks the cleaned sales data for accuracy, consistency, and completeness. If a record still contains missing, invalid, or inconsistent information, it is flagged for correction before being allowed to proceed to forecasting.
[0585] Chronological Arrangement – The system organizes validated sales records in time order so that the historical sales trend can be properly analyzed.
[0586] Forecast Preparation - The cleaned and ordered historical sales data are then stored and prepared for Holt’s forecasting, with MAPE used to measure prediction accuracy.
[0587] Error Handling - If an invalid or incomplete record is detected, the system flags it for correction before it can be processed further.
[0588] Forecasting Evaluation

To assess the accuracy of the forecasting model, the forecasted values are compared with the actual sales data using Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), which measures the average percentage difference between actual and forecasted values.

[0655] Legitimate Purpose - Data are collected exclusively for academic and research objectives. The information is utilized solely to analyze and improve the drugstore's inventory management and sales forecasting capabilities, ensuring no data is misappropriated for commercial or unrelated interests.
[0656] Proportionality - To minimize privacy risks, the researchers observe the principle of data minimization. Only information directly relevant to the study’s objectives is gathered, ensuring that the data collected is adequate, necessary, and not excessive.
[0657] To protect the integrity of the information gathered, the study implements robust data management protocols.
[0658] Anonymization - To prevent the identification of individual respondents, all personally identifiable information (PII) such as names, contact details, or specific identifying addresses is removed or replaced with unique identifiers. This ensures that individual survey responses cannot be linked back to the participants.
[0659] Secure Storage - All raw data files are stored in a centralized, encrypted, and password protected repository. This digital storage environment is designed to prevent unauthorized access, accidental alteration, or premature disclosure.
[0660] Restricted Access – Access to the collected data shall be limited exclusively to the primary researchers of the study. Third parties, including management staff not directly involved in the research, shall not be permitted to access raw survey responses or participant records.
[0661] Data Retention and Disposal - A clear retention policy is in place. Upon the conclusion and validation of the final research report, digital data will be permanently deleted, and any physical documentation will be securely shredded.
[0662] These measures provide a comprehensive framework that protects participant interests while ensuring that the research remains credible and legally sound throughout the implementation of the Pharxmaco Drugstore system
